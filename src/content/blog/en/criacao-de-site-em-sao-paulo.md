Search for "website design in São Paulo" and you'll find large agencies, small studios, freelancers, website builders promising a site in an afternoon and ads from all of them. In Brazil's largest city, there is no shortage of options. What's missing is a clear way to compare options that look the same in an ad and are very different in a contract.

This guide covers the types of provider, the questions that separate a serious proposal from a vague one, what to get in writing, the red flags and why hiring someone who knows São Paulo helps with local SEO.

## Types of website design providers in São Paulo

No single type of provider is right for everyone. There's the type that fits what your business needs, your timeline and how involved you want to be.

| Type | How it usually works | Good for | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Agency | A team with many roles: account management, design, development, content, media | Broad projects covering brand, campaigns and website at once | You may talk to an account manager and never to the people building the site; confirm who does the work |
| Freelancer | One professional handles all or part of the project | Small, well-defined projects with a flexible timeline | Dependence on a single person; agree on what happens if they become unavailable |
| Website builder | You build the site with templates and a visual editor, paying a subscription | People who want to start on their own with few pages | The site lives on the platform; customization, speed and technical SEO have limits |
| Studio | A lean team focused on websites and digital products, talking directly to the client | Custom websites with fixed scope, technical SEO and integrations | Confirm capacity to deliver on time and support after launch |

A practical way to decide is to answer three questions before asking for quotes:

1. **Who will write the copy?** If nobody at your company has time, rule out providers who don't produce content.
2. **Does the site need to talk to other systems?** CRM, sales WhatsApp, scheduling, ERP. If so, you need someone who develops, not just someone who assembles.
3. **Do you want to own the site?** If so, prefer providers who hand over the code and access at the end of the project.

## What to ask before hiring

Take these questions to any conversation, whether with a website design company in São Paulo or a freelancer. The answers say more than the portfolio:

- **Who exactly will work on my project?** Names and roles of the people designing, writing and coding.
- **Which pages are included?** Ask for the list, page by page, with what each one will contain.
- **Who writes the copy and who provides the photos?**
- **What does "SEO" mean in this proposal?** The answer should list concrete items: titles and descriptions, heading structure, sitemap, structured data, Search Console set up.
- **How do you measure mobile speed?** A good answer mentions Core Web Vitals and tools like PageSpeed Insights, not just "the site is fast".
- **Where does the form go?** E-mail, CRM, the sales team's WhatsApp.
- **How many revision rounds are included, and how are extra changes billed?**
- **What happens after launch?** Maintenance, fixes, security updates, new content.
- **If I already have a site, how will the migration work?** The answer needs to cover redirects from the old URLs.

If the provider can't answer clearly, they probably don't know what they'll deliver either. To understand how to compare prices across proposals, the article on [how much a business website costs](/blog/quanto-custa-site-institucional) explains what drives the price.

## What to require in the contract

A good conversation doesn't replace a contract. These items need to be in writing, whatever the type of provider:

**Domain in your company's name.** The domain must be registered under your company's CNPJ (Brazil's business tax ID), with admin access for you. A domain in the provider's name is the most common reason businesses get stuck with whoever built their site.

**Code and access handed over.** At the end of the project, you receive the source code and access to hosting, domain, Google Analytics and Search Console. If the site lives on the provider's platform, the contract must say what happens if you cancel.

**Fixed scope.** A list of pages, features, integrations and each side's responsibilities. "Responsive business website" is not a scope.

**A dated timeline.** Delivery date, milestones and what each side has to deliver for the deadline to hold. A deadline that depends on your copy must say when the copy is due.

**Technical SEO spelled out.** Titles and descriptions per page, clean URLs, sitemap, structured data, indexing checked in Search Console. If it only says "SEO included", ask for details.

**Speed and mobile.** A commitment to deliver the site measured on mobile, with Core Web Vitals checked. The guide on [slow websites and Core Web Vitals](/blog/site-lento-core-web-vitals) explains each metric.

**Recurring costs listed.** Hosting, domain renewal, e-mail, licenses and maintenance, item by item.

**Content ownership.** Copy, photos and layouts produced for the project belong to your company.

## Red flags

Some signs show up before the proposal. Be wary when you see:

- **A promise of Google's first page.** Nobody controls rankings. A provider can guarantee a technically sound, well-written site, not a position.
- **A one-line scope.** If you don't know what you'll get, you can't hold anyone to it.
- **A domain registered by the provider "to make things easier".**
- **No questions about your business.** A provider who doesn't ask who you sell to, where and what the site should generate is selling a template, not a project.
- **A portfolio without working links.** Ask to open the sites on a phone, in front of you.
- **Full payment upfront, with no milestones.**
- **Silence about what comes after.** If nobody mentions maintenance, there isn't any.
- **Pressure to sign today.** A good proposal is still good tomorrow.

## Why hiring someone from São Paulo helps with local SEO

A website can be built from anywhere. But when your customers are in São Paulo, someone who knows the city tends to get the details that matter on Google and in conversion right sooner.

**Neighborhood and regional pages.** For people searching, São Paulo isn't a single city. Someone in the East Zone searches for "dentista no Tatuapé", not "dentist in São Paulo". Businesses serving companies on Avenida Paulista, Faria Lima or in Alphaville talk to different audiences. A well-planned site has pages for the areas the business really serves, with specific information: how to get there, nearby neighborhoods served, parking, public transport. Copying the same page and swapping only the neighborhood name doesn't work and may be seen as low-quality content.

**Market knowledge.** Competition in São Paulo changes a lot from one neighborhood to another and from one industry to another. Someone who knows the city understands how your customers search, which local terms they use and where effort pays off.

**Consistency with your Google Business Profile.** Address, service area and website pages need to tell the same story. The guide on [how to show up on Google Maps](/blog/seo-local-google-maps) shows how to align your profile and website.

**In-person meetings are possible.** Much of the work can be done remotely, but sometimes sitting at the same table helps: the discovery meeting, the layout approval, the photo session at your location. With a provider in the city, that's easy to arrange.

**Time zone, holidays and pace.** It sounds minor, but working on the same calendar, including municipal holidays, avoids communication delays.

None of this replaces technical skill. A local provider who doesn't deliver technical SEO, speed and code you own doesn't help. The ideal combination is knowledge of the city plus a clear process.

## Professional website design in São Paulo: what can't be missing

Whoever you hire, a professional website for a São Paulo business needs:

- **One page per main service**, explaining the problem it solves, how it works and who it's for.
- **Regional pages**, when the business serves different parts of the city or Greater São Paulo.
- **Identical address, phone and hours** across the site and on your Google Business Profile.
- **Structured data** of type `LocalBusiness` or a specific subtype.
- **Mobile speed** that is measured, not just promised.
- **Form and WhatsApp connected** to sales, with leads tracked in Google Analytics 4.
- **A privacy policy and cookie banner** compliant with Brazil's LGPD.
- **Copy written for customers and for search**, without forced keyword repetition.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need to hire a São Paulo company to build a site for São Paulo customers?

It isn't required. But a provider who knows the city tends to plan neighborhood and regional pages better, understands how your customers search and can meet in person when needed. What matters most is still the process: fixed scope, technical SEO and code handed over.

### Agency or freelancer: which is better?

It depends on the project. An agency makes sense when the site is part of a bigger brand and media effort. A freelancer can handle small, well-defined projects well. Studios sit in between: a lean team, direct contact and a focus on websites and digital products. Compare by scope and contract, not by label.

### Is a website builder a good option?

It can be for getting started, with few pages and no integrations. The limits show up when you need speed, finer technical SEO, regional pages at scale or integration with other systems. And the site stays on the platform only as long as you pay the subscription.

### What's most important to require in the contract?

The domain under your company's CNPJ, code and access handed over, a fixed scope with a list of pages, a dated timeline and recurring costs listed item by item. Without these, it's hard to enforce what was agreed or to switch providers later.

### Do neighborhood pages really help on Google?

They help when they have truly specific content for each area and match places the business really serves. Pages copied with only the neighborhood name swapped don't help and can hurt how Google perceives the site's quality.
