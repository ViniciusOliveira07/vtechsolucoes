Putting a website online is easy. Having a website that shows up on Google when someone searches for what you sell is another story. Most company websites stay invisible because they were built backwards: design first, then copy and, if there's time left, Google.

This guide reverses the order. It shows, step by step, how to build a professional website designed from day one to be found and to turn visits into quote requests.

## Why so many company websites don't show up on Google

Before the step by step, it helps to know the most common reasons:

- **One page for everything.** If all your services are in the same text, Google has no specific page to show for each search.
- **Copy about the company, not the customer's problem.** Nobody searches for "integrated excellence solutions." People search for "commercial AC maintenance in Denver."
- **A slow website or a poor mobile experience.** Google evaluates the mobile version of your site and takes the visitor's experience into account.
- **Content Google can't read.** Text inside images, pages rendered only in the browser or pages blocked from search crawlers by mistake.
- **Nobody told Google.** Without Google Search Console and a sitemap, a new website can take a while to be discovered, and nobody tracks the errors.

## Step 1: define what the website needs to sell

Start with the business, not the layout. Answer in writing:

- Which services bring in the most margin and which do you want to sell more of?
- Who buys: job title, industry, company size, region?
- What does that customer need to know to request a quote?
- What action do you want them to take on the website: request a quote, message you on WhatsApp, book a visit?

These answers define the pages, the copy and the calls to action. A website without a clear goal becomes a pretty storefront nobody uses.

## Step 2: find out how your customers search

For each service, list the terms a customer would type into Google. Think about real variations:

- **The service and the area:** "accounting for medical practices in Austin".
- **The problem:** "how to reduce late HOA payments".
- **The comparison:** "custom software or off-the-shelf ERP".
- **Price and timeline:** "how much does a company website cost".

Google Ads Keyword Planner, the suggestions in the search bar itself and the "People also ask" box help you find variations and volume. Favor specific terms: they have less competition and attract people who are closer to buying.

## Step 3: plan the page structure

With your terms in hand, each important group of searches gets its own page. A structure that works for most service businesses:

- **Home page:** what you do, for whom, your main proof points and the path to each service.
- **One page per service:** the problem, how it works, who it's for, case studies, FAQs and a call to request a quote.
- **Location pages**, if you serve different cities and have truly specific content for each one. Avoid copying the same page and changing only the city name.
- **About:** history, team, company registration number, address and certifications.
- **Case studies or portfolio:** results with numbers.
- **Blog:** answers to the questions people have before they buy.
- **Contact:** short form, WhatsApp, phone, address and map.

Short, descriptive URLs help. `/servicos/manutencao-preventiva` says much more than `/pagina?id=27`.

## Step 4: write each page for one search

Each page needs one main focus and a complete answer to it:

- **A unique title and description**, with the main term and a reason to click. They are what appears in Google's results.
- **A single H1** that clearly states the topic, and subheadings (H2, H3) that organize the content.
- **Copy that really answers the question:** what it is, who it's for, how it works, how long it takes and what's included. Pages with three lines and a photo stand little chance against thorough competitors.
- **Internal links** between related pages, so visitors and Google understand the path.
- **Lightweight images with alt text** describing what they show.

Google itself recommends creating helpful, reliable, people-first content. Writing for your customer, with examples and concrete information, is also the best SEO strategy.

## Step 5: take care of technical SEO

This is the invisible part that decides whether everything else pays off:

- **Speed and Core Web Vitals.** These are Google's metrics for loading and usability experience. The "good" thresholds are: largest visible element loaded within 2.5 seconds (LCP), response to interactions within 200 milliseconds (INP) and a layout that doesn't "jump" while loading (CLS below 0.1). You can measure them with [PageSpeed Insights](https://pagespeed.web.dev/).
- **Mobile first.** Google indexes the mobile version. Everything on the desktop version needs to exist and work on mobile.
- **HTML that crawlers can read.** Server-rendered websites deliver content ready for Google, without relying on it to run JavaScript.
- **HTTPS and your own domain**, with a single main address (with or without "www", never both).
- **A correct sitemap and robots.txt**, indicating what should be indexed.
- **Structured data (Schema.org)** for organization, local business, articles and FAQs. It helps Google understand who you are and what each page contains.
- **Google Search Console** set up from day one, to submit the sitemap and track errors, searches and clicks.

## Step 6: set up your Google Business Profile

If you serve a specific area, your Google Business Profile (formerly Google My Business) is as important as your website. It is what shows up on the map and in the side panel of search results.

Fill in the category, services, hours, real photos and the link to your website. Keep your name, address and phone number identical on your website, your profile and your social media. And ask satisfied clients for reviews: they influence the decision of people searching and your local visibility.

## Step 7: turn visits into contacts

Showing up on Google without converting is a waste. On every page:

- **A clear, visible call to action** that matches the page ("request a maintenance quote," not a generic "learn more").
- **A short form:** name, phone, email and one question that qualifies the request. Every extra field reduces the number of submissions.
- **One-tap WhatsApp**, with the message already filled in.
- **An entry offer**, such as an audit or a free resource, for people who aren't ready to request a quote yet.
- **A fast response.** A lead that comes in through the website goes cold quickly. Automatic alerts to the sales team make a difference.

## Step 8: publish content regularly

A blog with articles that answer your customers' real questions expands the searches you show up for and demonstrates expertise. Two good, in-depth articles a month are worth more than ten generic posts. Each article should lead the reader to a service page or an offer.

## Step 9: measure and adjust

With Google Search Console and Google Analytics 4, track every month:

- Which searches the website shows up for and how many clicks it gets.
- Which pages bring in visits and which bring in contacts.
- Where contacts come from: organic search, social media, ads or referrals.

A page that shows up a lot but gets few clicks needs a better title. A page with visits but no contacts needs a stronger offer or call to action.

## How long it takes to show up on Google

A new website is usually discovered and indexed within a few days or a few weeks, especially once the sitemap is submitted through Search Console. Showing up in searches for your company name happens quickly. Gaining positions in service searches, especially competitive ones, takes months and depends on content, competition and the authority the website builds up. Specific searches, with service and location, usually pay off sooner.

Be wary of anyone who promises "Google's first page in a week." Nobody controls Google's rankings. What can be guaranteed is a technically sound website, with the right content and measurement to correct course.

## Website builder or custom website?

| | Website builder | Custom website |
|---|---|---|
| Upfront cost | Lower | Higher |
| Speed | Depends on the template; often heavy on mobile | Designed for Core Web Vitals |
| Technical SEO | Basic, with limited control | Full control over structure, structured data and rendering |
| Integrations | Limited to what the platform offers | Integrates with CRM, ERP, WhatsApp and automations |
| Ownership | You "rent" the platform | The code and the domain are yours |
| Best for | Getting started fast, with a few pages | Companies that want to generate opportunities through their website |

There is no single answer. If the website is just a business card, a website builder does the job. If it needs to bring in customers, integrate with your sales process and grow, a [custom website](/servicos/sites-institucionais) usually pays for itself.

## Checklist before you launch

- One page for each main service
- A unique title and description on every page
- One H1 per page
- A fast, complete website on mobile
- HTTPS, your own domain and a single main address
- Sitemap submitted to Google Search Console
- Structured data for your company
- Google Business Profile with your website link
- A call to action and a short form on every page
- Google Analytics 4 tracking contacts

## Frequently asked questions

### How much does a professional business website cost?

It depends on the number of pages, content production and integrations. A lean company website costs much less than a portal with a client area. More important than price is comparing what each proposal delivers: technical SEO, speed, content and measurement.

### Do I need a blog to show up on Google?

It's not required, but it helps a lot. Service pages show up for people who already want to hire. A blog reaches people who are still researching the problem, and that's where many competitors are absent.

### Can I switch websites without losing my Google rankings?

Yes, with care: map the old URLs, set up 301 redirects to the new ones and monitor Search Console after the switch. Migrating without redirects is one of the most common ways to lose traffic.

### Is it better to invest in SEO or Google Ads?

The two complement each other. Ads bring immediate results while the budget lasts. SEO takes longer, but the traffic keeps coming in with no cost per click. Many companies start with ads and reduce their dependence on them as organic traffic grows.
