Even with a referral in hand, people looking for a lawyer search on Google, open the firm's website and try to figure out whether that professional handles their kind of problem. If the site doesn't exist or doesn't explain the practice areas, they move on to the next result.

At the same time, the legal profession in Brazil has its own advertising rules, set by the OAB (Ordem dos Advogados do Brasil, the Brazilian Bar Association). What works on a store's website can be an ethics violation on a law firm's. This guide covers the Brazilian rules: what OAB legal marketing rules allow and ban, what a lawyer's website needs and how to get the firm found on Google without crossing the line.

> **Important:** this article is informational and is not a legal opinion. The rules cited were checked against the official texts of the OAB Federal Council, but interpreting specific cases is up to the OAB. Before publishing, confirm any doubts with your OAB state section (seccional).

## The rules that apply to a law firm's website in Brazil

Two OAB Federal Council rules form the basis of legal advertising in Brazil:

- **The OAB Code of Ethics and Discipline** ([Resolução nº 02/2015](https://www.oab.org.br/leisnormas/legislacao/resolucoes/02-2015)), which devotes a whole chapter to professional advertising, in articles 39 to 47.
- **[Provimento nº 205/2021](https://www.oab.org.br/leisnormas/legislacao/provimentos/205-2021)**, which deals with advertising and information in the legal profession, replaced the old Provimento 94/2000 and includes an annex with criteria for social media, ads, chatbots and other digital tools.

The starting point is article 39 of the Code of Ethics: a lawyer's professional advertising must be merely informational, discreet and sober, and must not amount to soliciting clients or commercializing the profession. Provimento 205/2021 repeats this logic in article 3 and, right in article 1, makes it clear that legal marketing is allowed as long as it is compatible with ethical principles.

In practice, a lawyer can and should have a website. What changes is the tone and the content: the site tells people who the firm is, what it does and how to reach it. It doesn't sell, promise or pressure.

## What Provimento 205/2021 allows

The provimento brought more clarity to the digital world. Among the permitted points that matter directly for a website:

- **Legal content marketing.** Articles, videos and informational material about legal topics are allowed, including active or passive advertising and paid or unpaid ads, as long as there is no commercialization, no client solicitation and no excessive use of financial resources (article 4).
- **Qualifications and titles.** Lawyers can list their education and titles, provided they are true and can be proven when the OAB asks, and name the law firm they belong to (article 4, §1).
- **Logo and photos.** A logo and images, including photos of the lawyers and the office, are allowed. Using the OAB's official logo and symbols is not (article 5, §2).
- **Videos and live streams.** Taking part in lives and debates is allowed, without using specific cases or presenting results (article 5, §3).
- **Chatbot on the site.** The annex allows chatbots to make communication easier, answer first questions and collect information, as long as they don't replace the lawyer's personal involvement and responsibility.
- **Search keywords.** The annex also allows buying keywords on search engines such as Google, when the ad answers a search started by the person and the keywords respect ethical principles.

## What is banned in legal advertising

The bans are spread across the Code of Ethics and the provimento. These are the ones that show up most on websites:

| Ban | Where it is |
|---|---|
| Mentioning fees, payment methods, free services or discounts as a way to attract clients | Provimento 205/2021, art. 3, I |
| Information that misleads or causes harm | Provimento 205/2021, art. 3, II |
| Advertising a specialty without a certified title or recognized expertise | Provimento 205/2021, art. 3, III |
| Persuasive, self-promoting or comparative expressions | Provimento 205/2021, art. 3, IV |
| Directly urging people to sue or to hire; personal promotion | Provimento 205/2021, art. 3, §1 |
| Citing court decisions and results obtained in cases handled | Provimento 205/2021, art. 4, §2 |
| Promising results or using specific cases in active advertising | Provimento 205/2021, art. 6 |
| Showing off assets such as cars, trips and hotels | Provimento 205/2021, art. 6, sole paragraph |
| Paying to appear in rankings, awards or honors | Provimento 205/2021, art. 5, §1 |
| Advertising legal services together with other activities | Code of Ethics, art. 40, IV, and Provimento 205/2021, art. 8 |
| Publishing, or allowing others to publish, client and case lists | Code of Ethics, art. 42, IV |
| Direct mail and flyers aimed at soliciting clients | Code of Ethics, art. 40, VI |

On a website, these are some things that often show up and shouldn't:

- **"Free consultation", "first meeting at no cost" or a price list.** Mentioning free services or fees as bait to attract clients is banned.
- **"The best family lawyers in town."** That's self-promotion and comparison.
- **"We won the case and the client got compensation."** That's disclosing a result in a specific case.
- **"We guarantee your retirement benefit fast."** That's a promise of results.
- **Client testimonials describing how their case was solved.** They mix client lists, specific cases and results.
- **Buttons like "File your lawsuit now".** They directly urge people to sue.

## Active and passive advertising: why the website is the right place

The annex to Provimento 205/2021 separates two concepts that help explain why the website is the safest piece of legal marketing:

- **Active advertising** reaches an undetermined number of people, even if they didn't look for the information.
- **Passive advertising** reaches only people who looked for the information or agreed to receive it.

People who reach a firm's website have usually searched for a topic or for the firm itself. That makes it the best place to explain practice areas in depth and to publish informational legal content. It doesn't exempt the site from the rules: the content bans still apply.

## What a lawyer's website needs

A law firm website that follows the rules still has to be found and build trust. For that, it needs a clear structure.

**A focused home page.** Say who the firm is, which practice areas it covers and where it works. "Family law firm in Santo André" tells people more than a slogan.

**One page per practice area.** This is what matters most on Google. A single page listing every area shows up in few searches. One page for family law, another for estates, another for consumer law, each explaining what situations the firm handles, how the service works and which documents are usually requested at the first meeting, shows up in many more searches and helps visitors recognize their own situation.

**A team page with OAB registration numbers.** Present each lawyer with name, OAB registration number and state section, education and areas of practice. The Code of Ethics requires the name and registration number on business cards and office material (article 44), and carrying the same information to the website is good transparency practice. Academic titles only go in if they are true and provable. For a law firm (sociedade de advogados), also show the firm's registration.

**Informational articles.** The blog is where legal marketing meets SEO. Plain-language articles on topics clients search for, such as how an out-of-court probate works or what to do when a flight is canceled, are allowed and bring qualified visits. The content must be technical and informational, without urging people to sue, without citing client cases and without ending in a "hire us now" call. What about contact details next to an article? The Code of Ethics bans giving an address and phone number in columns and articles in the press and online, allowing e-mail (article 40, V). The provimento treated all of the firm's contact details and channels as equivalent to e-mail, including website, social media, messaging apps and logo, as long as they are informational and sober (article 4, §3). A discreet signature with the firm's channels is different from an invitation to hire.

**A discreet, complete contact page.** Address, phone, e-mail, office hours, map and a simple form. A WhatsApp button for an initial question is fine, as long as the default message is neutral, like "Hi, I'd like to talk to the firm".

**Sober design.** Readable type, restrained colors and real photos of the office and team, instead of stock images of gavels and scales.

## Local SEO for law firms

Most searches for a lawyer have a local component: "labor lawyer in Guarulhos", "family lawyer near me". To show up for them:

- **Google Business Profile.** Create or claim the firm's profile, pick the most specific category and keep address, phone and hours identical to the website. When replying to reviews, never discuss the client's case: thank them and invite them to continue privately, protecting professional confidentiality.
- **Pages per practice area and region.** If the firm serves more than one city, a page per location with truly specific information helps Google understand where you work. The guide on [how to show up on Google Maps](/blog/seo-local-google-maps) walks through the steps.
- **Structured data.** Mark up the site with the Schema.org `LegalService` or `Attorney` type, with name, address, phone and hours.
- **Mobile speed.** People looking for a lawyer on their phone, often worried, won't wait for a slow site to load.

About Google Ads: the provimento's annex allows buying keywords when the ad answers a search started by the person, but the ad copy is subject to the same bans.

## LGPD on a law firm's website

The site collects data through the form, WhatsApp and cookies, and people writing to a law firm often describe a personal problem in their very first message. So the site must comply with Brazil's [General Data Protection Law (LGPD)](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/_ato2015-2018/2018/lei/l13709.htm):

- **A clear privacy policy** saying what data is collected, why, for how long and how people can request access or deletion.
- **A short form.** Ask only what's needed for the first contact: name, how to reach them and a summary of the matter. Say that case details will be handled during the consultation.
- **A cookie banner** with a real option to refuse anything non-essential.
- **A secure connection (HTTPS)**, with the form sent to a channel the firm controls, not a shared personal inbox.

## Checklist before publishing

Use this list in your final review, together with reading the rules:

- Does each practice area have its own page, and does each lawyer appear with name and OAB registration number?
- Are the titles and specialties listed true and provable?
- Is the site free of fees, free offers, promises, won cases, case testimonials and superlatives?
- Are there no client lists, OAB logo or displays of wealth?
- Are the privacy policy and cookie banner published?
- Have the doubtful points been confirmed with your OAB section?

## Frequently asked questions

### Can a lawyer in Brazil have a website?

Yes. Provimento 205/2021 allows legal marketing and professional advertising, as long as it is informational, discreet and sober, without client solicitation and without commercialization.

### Can I show the consultation fee on the site?

Not as a way to attract clients. Provimento 205/2021 bans mentioning fees, payment methods, free services or discounts as a way to solicit clients. Fees should be discussed directly with the client.

### Can I publish client testimonials?

It's risky. The rules ban citing results obtained in cases handled, and the Code of Ethics bans publishing client and case lists. A testimonial about a solved case usually falls into both. When in doubt, don't publish it and ask your OAB section.

### Can I call myself a "specialist" on the site?

Only with a certified title or recognized expertise in the area. Otherwise, use "practice areas" or "we work with", which describe the work without advertising a specialty.

### Does this article replace asking the OAB?

No. It summarizes the official rules to guide the creation of the site, but it is not a legal opinion. Interpreting specific cases is up to the OAB, and your state section is the channel for confirming doubts before publishing.
