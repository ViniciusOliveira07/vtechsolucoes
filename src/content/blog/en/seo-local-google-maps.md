Someone in your city searches for "dentist near me" or "AC repair in Austin." Google shows a map with three businesses, each one's reviews and a button to call. If your business isn't in that box, for a lot of people it simply doesn't exist.

Showing up on Google Maps doesn't depend on luck or on paying for ads. It depends on a set of adjustments to your Google Business Profile, your website and the way your business appears across the web. This guide shows what to do, what to avoid and how to measure whether it's working.

## How Google decides who shows up on the map

Google itself explains that local results depend on three factors:

- **Relevance.** How well your profile matches what the person searched for. A well-filled category, services and description increase relevance.
- **Distance.** How close your business is to the searcher, or to the location named in the search. You don't control this one.
- **Prominence.** How well known your business is. Reviews, mentions on other websites, links and your website's strength in organic search all count here.

The official documentation is in [tips to improve your local ranking on Google](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091). The rest of this guide is about working on relevance and prominence.

## Step 1: claim and verify your Google Business Profile

Your Google Business Profile (formerly Google My Business) is the listing that shows up on Maps and in the side panel of search results. Search for your company name: the profile may already exist, created by a customer or by Google itself. If so, claim it. If it doesn't exist, create it at business.google.com and complete verification.

Choose the right type of profile:

- **With a visible address**, if customers come to you: a store, clinic or office that receives clients.
- **Service area**, if you go to the customer: technicians, service providers, deliveries. In this case, the address is hidden and you list the cities or regions you serve.

## Step 2: choose the right categories

The primary category is one of the items that weighs most on relevance. Choose the most specific one that describes your main business. "Dental clinic" is better than "Medical clinic" for a dentist's office.

Then add secondary categories for other services you actually offer. Don't add categories just to show up in more searches: a category that doesn't match the business confuses both Google and the customer.

A practical way to decide: search for your service in your city and look at the categories of the competitors at the top.

## Step 3: fill in services, description and attributes

The more complete the profile, the more searches it can answer:

- **Services or products**, one by one, with a short description. This is where the variations customers search for come in.
- **Business description**, explaining what you do, for whom and where. Write for the customer, without repeating keywords for no reason.
- **Business hours**, including holidays. Wrong hours lead to negative reviews.
- **Phone, website and booking link**, when available.
- **Attributes**, such as accessibility, parking, online service or payment methods.

## Step 4: post real photos and updates

Real photos of your storefront, your space, your team and the work you deliver help customers choose and show that the business actually exists. A storefront photo helps people who come to you find the place. Avoid stock photos: customers notice.

Profile updates (posts) are for showing news, offers, events and recent work. They don't replace a well-filled profile, but they show the business is active. A simple routine, such as one update a week with a photo of a finished job, already changes how visitors perceive your profile.

## Step 5: ask for reviews and reply to all of them

Reviews weigh on prominence and, above all, on the decision of people comparing options. Nobody calls the business with a low rating and three old comments when the one next to it has dozens of recent reviews.

**How to ask for reviews:**

- Ask right after delivery, when the customer is satisfied. That's the best moment.
- Use the short review link your Business Profile generates and send it by WhatsApp or email.
- Put a QR code on the counter, the receipt or your business card.
- Make it easy: tell the customer it takes a minute and, when it makes sense, suggest they mention which service they hired.

**What not to do:** offer a discount, a gift or any benefit in exchange for a review, ask only the customers you know are happy or write fake reviews. All of this violates Google's policies and can lead to reviews being removed or the profile being suspended.

**How to reply:**

- Reply to all of them, positive and negative.
- On positive reviews, say thank you and mention the service when it feels natural.
- On negative reviews, reply calmly, acknowledge the problem, explain what was done and invite the person to continue the conversation privately. The next customer reads the reply, not just the author of the complaint.

## Step 6: keep your NAP identical everywhere

NAP stands for name, address and phone number. Google cross-checks this information in several places to confirm that the business is real and is where it says it is. Inconsistencies weaken that trust.

Make sure your NAP is exactly the same:

- On your Google Business Profile.
- In your website's footer and contact page.
- On social media.
- In directories and associations.

"St." in one place and "Street" in another isn't a serious problem. An old phone number, the previous office's address or a different name on each channel are. When you change your address or phone number, update everything at once.

## Step 7: create location pages on your website

Your profile brings customers to you, but your website reinforces relevance. If you serve more than one city or have more than one location, create a page for each, with:

- That location's address, phone number and hours.
- An embedded map.
- The services offered there.
- Truly specific information: directions, neighborhoods served, photos of the location, case studies from local customers.

Avoid copying the same page and changing only the city name. Pages like that don't help and may be seen as low-quality content. The website link on your Business Profile should point to the matching location page, not always to the home page. If your website doesn't have this structure yet, the guide on [how to build a professional website that ranks on Google](/blog/como-criar-site-profissional-que-aparece-no-google) shows how to set up the pages.

## Step 8: use LocalBusiness structured data

Structured data is code invisible to visitors that describes your business to Google in a standard format (Schema.org). For local businesses, the type is `LocalBusiness` or a more specific subtype, such as `Dentist` or `AutoRepair`.

The most important fields:

- `name`, `address`, `telephone` and `url`, matching your profile's NAP.
- `openingHoursSpecification`, with your business hours.
- `geo`, with latitude and longitude.
- `areaServed`, if you serve a region.

The official reference is [Google's local business structured data documentation](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/local-business). After publishing, validate it with Google's Rich Results Test.

## Step 9: build local citations

A local citation is any mention of your business name, address and phone number on another website: directories, city guides, chambers of commerce, trade associations, supplier and partner websites. They reinforce prominence and confirm your NAP.

Prioritize quality and relevance: your industry's directory, your city's chamber of commerce and the sites your customers actually use. Mass listings on unknown directories take work and bring little return. Keep a spreadsheet with each listing, login and the data used, so you can update everything when something changes.

## Common mistakes that hurt or suspend your profile

Some shortcuts seem to work at first and cost a lot later:

| Mistake | Why it's a problem |
|---|---|
| Keywords in the business name ("Silva Auto Repair Cheap Mechanic Austin 24h") | Violates Google's guidelines; the name must be the real name used on your storefront and documents |
| Fake address, virtual office or PO box | Violates the guidelines; the profile can be suspended |
| Multiple profiles for the same business at the same address | Treated as duplicates; profiles can be removed |
| A category that doesn't match the business | Lowers relevance and confuses customers |
| Bought, fake or discount-for-review reviews | Violates review policies; they can be removed and the profile penalized |
| Different phone and address on the website and the profile | Weakens Google's trust in your information |
| An abandoned profile with unanswered reviews | Makes the business look careless or closed |

The full rules are in the [guidelines for representing your business on Google](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177). Read them before changing your name, address or category.

## How to measure local SEO results

If you don't measure, you won't know what worked. Track every month:

- **Business Profile performance:** how many people saw the profile, through which searches, how many called, asked for directions or clicked to your website.
- **Website link with UTM.** Add UTM parameters to the website link on your profile. That way, Google Analytics 4 separates the visits that came from Maps.
- **Google Search Console:** searches with a city name or "near me" that bring clicks to your location pages.
- **Map position by area.** Search for your service from different neighborhoods. Position changes depending on where the searcher is, so a single search from your office doesn't show the real picture.
- **Incoming leads.** Ask customers how they found you and record it in your CRM.

Compare month to month, not day to day. Changes to your profile and website usually take a few weeks to show up on the map.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long does it take to show up on Google Maps?

After verification, your profile starts appearing in searches for your business name fairly quickly. Ranking near the top for service searches depends on local competition, distance and your business's prominence, and usually takes weeks or months of consistent work.

### Do I need a physical address to show up on Google Maps?

Not necessarily. Businesses that serve customers at their location can create a service-area profile with the address hidden. What isn't allowed is using a fake address, a virtual office or a PO box to simulate a physical presence.

### Can I pay to show up at the top of Google Maps?

You can advertise, and ads show up labeled as sponsored. The organic position on the map can't be bought. It depends on relevance, distance and prominence.

### Does a negative review hurt my profile?

A single negative review, answered well, does little harm and can even add credibility. The problem is a pile of bad reviews without replies. Reviews that violate Google's policies, such as insults or spam, can be reported from your profile.

### Do I need a website for local SEO?

Your profile works without a website, but it's weaker. A website reinforces relevance, allows location pages and structured data and is where customers go when they want to learn more before calling.
