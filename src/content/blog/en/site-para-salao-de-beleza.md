A woman moves to a new neighborhood, needs her nails done on Saturday and picks up her phone. She doesn't open Instagram to look for a studio she's never heard of. She types "nail salon near me" or "hair salon in Wicker Park" into Google. The salons that show up there are the ones she'll consider. Everyone else is out of the running.

A beauty salon website exists to win exactly that moment: getting found by people who don't know you yet and taking them, without friction, to a booked appointment. This guide covers what the website of a salon, nail studio, barbershop, hair studio or esthetics clinic needs, what usually gets in the way and how it works together with Instagram and Google Maps.

## Why Instagram alone doesn't bring new clients from Google

Instagram is great for people who already follow your salon. It shows your work, builds a relationship and reminds clients to come back. The problem is the other side: the client who doesn't know you exist yet.

When someone searches Google for a service nearby, it shows a map with business profiles and, right below it, website pages. Your Instagram content almost never shows up in those searches. Posts have no page titles, no text organized by service and neighborhood, and often can't even be fully viewed without logging in. To Google, a feed full of beautiful photos says very little about what you do and where you are.

There are other limits:

- **You don't own the channel.** An algorithm change or a blocked or hacked account, and all that work disappears overnight.
- **Information gets lost.** Prices, address, hours and the service list end up scattered across highlights and old captions. A new client gives up before finding them.
- **There's no page for each service.** Someone searching for "gel nail extensions" wants to land on a page about that, not on a general profile.

What works is the combination: a Google Business Profile to show up on the map, a website to show up in search and convince, and Instagram to show your day-to-day and keep clients close. ## What a beauty salon website needs

It doesn't have to be big. It has to be clear, fast and answer the questions a client asks before booking.

### A page for each main service

This is what makes the biggest difference on Google. Instead of a single "Services" page with a list, create a page for each service you want to sell more of: cuts and color, keratin treatments, highlights, gel nails, extensions, brow design, lash extensions, facials, waxing.

Each page should answer:

- What the service is and who it's for.
- How the appointment works, from start to finish.
- Roughly how long it takes, and what care the client needs before and after.
- Photos of real work for that service.
- Frequently asked questions about that service.
- A clearly visible booking button.

If your prices are fixed, showing the price or a "starting at" helps clients decide. If the price depends on a consultation, say so clearly and explain how it works.

### Real photos of your work

In beauty, clients buy with their eyes. A gallery of your salon's real work is worth more than any amount of text. A few guidelines:

- Organize photos by service, not in one big mixed gallery.
- Avoid stock photos: clients notice, and trust drops.
- Get permission from anyone who appears in a photo, especially when their face is visible.
- Compress images before publishing. Heavy photos make the site slow on phones, and that drives clients away.

### Address, map and directions

It sounds obvious, but many salon websites bury the address in the footer. Put your name, full address, phone number and hours somewhere easy to find, with an embedded map and a button that opens directions on the phone.

Explaining how to get there helps a lot: a landmark, whether there's parking nearby, whether you're inside a shopping center or upstairs, whether the space is accessible.

### Reviews and social proof

A new client wants to know what other clients thought. Show a few real reviews from your Google Business Profile on the site, with the name the person used when reviewing, and add a link for anyone who wants to read them all.

Don't make up testimonials or edit what a client wrote.

### About the salon and the team

Show who does the work. A team photo, each professional's name, specialty and training or courses taken. In beauty services, many clients choose the professional, not just the salon.

## Salon website with online booking: the shortest path to an appointment

The website doesn't need its own booking system. In most cases, the best move is to connect the site to what the salon already uses every day.

**Booking through WhatsApp or text.** This works well for smaller salons or those that prefer to chat before booking. The site's button should open the conversation with a pre-filled message that already says which page the client came from, for example: "Hi, I saw your website and want to book gel nails." If the message volume is high, you can automate triage and the most common replies, as we explain in our guide to an [AI agent on WhatsApp](/blog/agente-de-ia-no-whatsapp).

**A link to the online booking app the salon already uses.** If you already work with a booking app, the site should send clients straight to it, ideally to the right service. The client sees open slots, picks one and confirms without waiting for anyone to reply.

Whichever path you choose, a few rules apply:

- **A booking button on every page**, visible on a phone without scrolling.
- **One main action.** If the salon books through an online app, don't scatter several different paths across the site. Pick the main one and keep the other as an alternative.
- **The same link on the website, Instagram and Business Profile.** Clients arriving through any door find the same path.
- **Test it as a client.** Open the site on your phone, tap book and count the steps to a confirmed appointment. Every extra step is a client lost.

## Google Business Profile: your website works together with the map

For someone searching "salon near me," the first contact is usually the Google map, not the website. That's why your Google Business Profile needs to be complete and consistent with your site:

- **The right primary category**, as specific as possible: beauty salon, nail salon, barber shop, esthetician, depending on your main business.
- **Services listed one by one**, with the same names used on your website pages.
- **Real photos** of the storefront, the space and your work, updated often.
- **Website link** pointing to the right page, plus a booking link when you have one.
- **Identical name, address and phone number** on the profile, the website and Instagram.
- **Replies to reviews**, good and bad, always polite.

## Neighborhood-level local SEO: showing up for people nearby

A salon is a neighborhood business. Clients rarely cross town to get their nails done. So your website should make it clear, in text, where you are and who you serve.

- **Mention the neighborhood and city** in the homepage title, on service pages and in the copy, naturally. "Gel nails in Wicker Park" tells Google and the client exactly what they'll find.
- **Mention nearby areas** you really serve, such as the neighboring areas many of your clients come from. Don't create dozens of identical pages swapping only the neighborhood name: that doesn't help and can be seen as low-quality content.
- **If you have more than one location**, create a page for each, with that location's address, hours, team, photos and services.
- **Use structured data** of type `BeautySalon`, `NailSalon` or `HairSalon`, which are subtypes of `LocalBusiness` on Schema.org, with name, address, phone, hours and booking link. It's code clients don't see that helps Google understand your business.
- **Write page titles with search in mind.** "Brow design in Lincoln Park | Bella Studio" works better than "Services | Home."

## Mobile speed: where most salon websites lose the client

Almost every client will see your salon's website on a phone, often while walking down the street or on a work break. If the page is slow to load, she goes back to Google and taps the next result.

The most common culprits on salon websites are:

- **Huge photos**, exported straight from a camera or phone without compression.
- **Autoplay videos** at the top of the page.
- **Embedded Instagram feeds**, which load a lot of third-party code.
- **Heavy off-the-shelf themes**, packed with effects and plugins nobody uses.

## Common mistakes on salon and nail studio websites

| Mistake | Why it hurts |
|---|---|
| All services on a single page | The site doesn't show up in searches for specific services |
| Hidden or missing address | Clients can't tell if the salon is nearby and give up |
| Booking button only in the footer | On a phone, clients never reach it |
| Different phone numbers on the site, Instagram and Google | Confuses clients and weakens Google's trust |
| Stock photos | Clients notice and doubt the real work |

## Website for a nail studio, hair salon or spa: does anything change?

The structure is the same; the focus shifts.

- **Nail studio:** a strong gallery organized by technique (gel, acrylic, polish, nail art), an explanation of fills and how often to come back, and very fast booking. It's a recurring service, so make rebooking easy.
- **Hair salon:** separate pages for cuts, color, highlights, treatments and chemical services, with real, authorized before-and-after photos, and an explanation of when a consultation is needed before quoting a price.
- **Esthetics:** more explanatory text about each treatment, who it's for and aftercare, responsible language and no promise of guaranteed results. Health-related procedures may have advertising rules set by the licensing board of whoever performs them.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does a small salon need a website?

Yes, if you want new clients from Google. A simple site with a homepage, pages for your main services, a gallery, an about page, contact and booking is enough. The size of the salon matters less than how clear the information is.

### Can I just use the link in my Instagram bio?

As a complement, yes, but it doesn't replace a website. The bio link helps people who are already on your profile. The website helps people who don't know you exist yet and are searching on Google.

### Do I need a booking system on my website?

Not necessarily. The site can send clients to WhatsApp with a pre-filled message or to the online booking app the salon already uses. What matters is that the path is short and the same across every channel.

### Should I put prices on my website?

If your prices are fixed, showing them helps clients decide and filters out inquiries that don't fit your audience. If they depend on a consultation, explain why and show how the client can request a quote.

### How long does it take for the website to show up on Google?

Once it's published and submitted to Google Search Console, the site usually starts showing up in searches for the salon's name fairly quickly. Ranking well for service searches in your neighborhood depends on local competition and consistent work on the site and the Business Profile.
